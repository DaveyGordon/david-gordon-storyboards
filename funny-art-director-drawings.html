Funny art director drawings.

Before a storyboard can become a storyboard, somebody has to explain the idea. Sometimes they draw it.

A survey of important works produced in conference rooms, on phone calls and under conditions of extreme deadline pressure.

Collection highlight

A Man in a Gorilla Suit Starts Messing With the Man’s Computer

Anonymous Art Director, c. 2000s
Ink on office paper
Department of Illustration Collection

In this unusually lucid example of early twenty-first-century commercial iconography, the artist depicts a man in a gorilla suit interfering with another man’s computer. The precise nature of the interference is left unresolved.

Scholars remain divided as to whether the gorilla represents chaos, technology, the subconscious, or simply a man in a gorilla suit messing with a computer.

Artist’s instruction:
“A man in a gorilla suit starts messing with the man’s computer.”

Study in two movements

Guy on Laptop Is Racing Flip Board / Grave of Guy

Anonymous Art Director, c. 2000s
Graphite on office paper
Department of Illustration Collection

A compact two-panel narrative spanning competition, consequence and mortality. The second panel resolves the first with unusual decisiveness.

Notable detail: the deceased is identified simply as “John Henry.”

Fruit studies

Strawberry

Anonymous Art Director, c. 2000s
Red marker on office paper
Department of Illustration Collection

The fruit appears at right. The figure appears at left. The word STRAWBERRY anchors the composition and eliminates any remaining ambiguity.

Mobility studies

Bike / Skateboard / Scooter / Rollerblades

Anonymous Art Director
Ballpoint pen on office paper
Department of Illustration Collection

Four modes of transportation orbit a monumental cube. The cube itself is not identified.

Its silence is generally considered intentional.

Figurative work

Untitled (Head, Inverted)

Anonymous Art Director
Marker on office paper
Department of Illustration Collection

The figure’s body remains upright. Its head does not.

Scholars have been unable to establish whether this is an accident, a camera direction or a highly specific performance note.
